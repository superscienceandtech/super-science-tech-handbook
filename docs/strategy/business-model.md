# Business Model

**Status:** Draft based on the founder-confirmed three-pillar model.

## Pillar A — Client services

Potential engagement types (proposed, confirm actual catalogue):
- Discovery and technical consulting
- Web and full-stack application development
- Backend/API and database systems
- CRM and internal business platforms
- AI/ML integration and prototypes
- Maintenance, reliability, and technical improvements

Commercial terms, delivery commitments, supported technologies, and pricing are TBD.

## Pillar B — Own software products

Build products that the company owns and can maintain and improve over time.

For each product, document:
- Problem and target user
- Product owner and roadmap
- Product requirements and architecture
- Pricing and distribution model
- Security, privacy, support, and service-level commitments
- Unit economics and success metrics

Product names, launch dates, and revenue targets: TBD.

## Pillar C — AI research

Use a research lifecycle:
1. Define a specific question and review prior work.
2. Record a hypothesis and evaluation plan.
3. Check dataset rights, privacy, and ethical risks.
4. Establish baselines and appropriate metrics.
5. Run reproducible experiments.
6. Report results, limitations, and failure cases.
7. Decide whether to publish, prototype, productize, or stop.

Research priorities, publication approval process, and compute budget: TBD.

## How the pillars can reinforce one another

Client work can reveal recurring problems; product work can turn validated repeatable solutions into owned assets; research can create new capabilities. Client agreements, IP rights, confidentiality, and conflicts of interest must be reviewed before transferring code, data, or ideas between engagements.
